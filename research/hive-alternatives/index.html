Keep the movement. Change the meaning.

These are unofficial retheme proposals for John Yianni’s Hive, published by Gen42. They introduce no powers or balance changes. All pieces retain their original role, including the target’s placement deadline and the prohibition on moving before it is placed. Keep the One Hive connection rule, placement restrictions, freedom-to-move restrictions and stacking rules. Surround the opposing target’s six neighboring spaces to win; simultaneous encirclement is a draw. Use the official base-game rules for full play.

The following is a quick mapping, not a replacement rulebook. No expansion pieces are included. Names and illustrations are original proposals; this is not an official Hive product.

Original rolePer playerMovement reminder
Queen bee1One adjacent space.
Soldier ant3Any distance around the perimeter, subject to sliding.
Beetle2One space, including onto or off a stack.
Grasshopper3Straight jump over one or more contiguous pieces to the first empty space.
Spider2Exactly three sliding perimeter steps, without backtracking.

How to read the specimens: dark marks represent intended recesses in a flat top, not separate parts. The drawings communicate symbol ideas; they are not dimensioned CAD, toolpaths or guaranteed-printable geometry. Their orientation has no effect on legal movement. Names are mnemonics: no piece tows, carries, pushes, teleports through gaps or affects its neighbors. A three-step role always needs the original movement rule, even when its symbol suggests a creature or a tool.

Big symbols. Flat tops. Honest tolerances.

Design recommendation: a common hexagonal tile with a flat underside and recessed top symbol. Keep all engraving inside a central 26 mm circle so the outer deck stays continuous. Flat surfaces let every piece support a climber, without a sculpted socket. Avoid raised miniatures, magnets, snap fits and protruding feet in the first version.

FeatureStarting targetReason
Hex footprint40 mm across flats; 46.2 mm across cornersA generous icon area; a proposal, not official tile dimensions.
Tile height8.1 mm27 layers at a proposed 0.3 mm layer height.
Recess width / solid web / gapPrefer 2.4 mm; test down to 1.8 mmAt a 0.9 mm line width, 1.8 mm is roughly two extrusion widths. Width is not a guarantee of resolution.
Recess depth0.9 mm at 0.3 mm layersThree layers of depth, with a flat supporting deck around it.
Edge break0.6 mm chamfer as an initial trialReduce sharp edges; inspect bottom-edge spread and sliding.

These are untested CAD targets. Nozzle diameter, extrusion width and layer height are different parameters. Prusa’s profile guide explains that nozzle size alone does not set extrusion width. Start from your printer’s supported 0.8 mm profile; inspect the actual widths rather than assuming 0.8 mm lines. The 0.3 mm layer proposal is for symbol definition, not a speed claim.

Print underside-down, symbols up. Top recesses have no roof to bridge. Keep every line broad, every tip blunt and all body geometry connected. At the modeling stage widen tight corners and gaps in these sketches to meet the chosen minimum. Prusa’s modeling guidance ties thin-wall limits to extrusion paths and recommends test prints; the targets above are our design choices, not values certified by Prusa.

Make stacks and teams readable

Use two clearly contrasting filament colors, one per player, with identical piece geometry. For a tactile backup, trial one broad recessed groove on every side for team A and two separated grooves for team B; keep the same outer footprint, top and bottom. Side cues need their own print test and must not project into sliding paths. No multicolor printer is required. Test the symbols without paint under ordinary room lighting; if same-color recesses are hard to read, widen the icon or try a contrasting paint wipe.

Test both climbers on every other role, at all six hex alignments, and test a four-climber stack. A covered piece is immobilized under the ordinary Hive rules; do not treat a visible side mark as permission to move it. The top piece supplies stack ownership for placement. Side team cues help identify the layer below, but do not show its role. Covered targets still count for encirclement; remember covered identities. Optional broad side role marks need a separate legibility test. Inspect covered roles without changing the game position if players lose track. Check that stacks sit flat, do not rock over a recess and remain readable from a seated position.

Print five samples before twenty-two.

  1. Choose one theme. Space Salvage is the recommended starting point. Model five symbols on the same base; keep the table of original roles beside the board.
  2. Print a small width coupon. Compare 1.8, 2.4 and 3.0 mm grooves and remaining webs, using the actual nozzle, material and profile. Include the team side cue. Inspect toolpaths for missing or merged details before printing.
  3. Print one of each role. Check recognition in one color, touch comfort, edge-to-edge sliding and two-tile stacking. If two symbols are confused, change the shape rather than adding tiny decoration.
  4. Test mixed stacks. Add three more climbers for the tallest four-climber trial. Check stability, team recognition, all rotations and whether the edge chamfer causes unwanted rocking.
  5. Complete the set. Per color: target ×1, perimeter runner ×3, climber ×2, jumper ×3, three-step walker ×2. Play with the original rules before considering any gameplay variants.

Deliverable boundary: this page supplies five themes, 25 illustrated piece briefs and a manufacturing plan. No STL files, slicing results or physical print tests are claimed.

Evidence, judgment & council.

Council synthesis

Both GPT-6.1 Sol members favored Space Salvage and Clockwork City for distinct shapes and movement associations, and accepted the proposal as an exploration with untested print targets. Their recommendations informed the flat recessed layout, coarse feature targets, movement-only naming and stack-identity notes. The OpenRouter Free advisory review reinforced the need for simple silhouettes; its assumptions about raised relief and fine-detail limits were not adopted.

The council differed on some names and icon choices. This page keeps a ladder as the library climber to distinguish it from the target book, and uses three broad arm marks as an octopus emblem. Living Landscapes remains the most abstract option. These are editorial choices; physical readability remains open.
